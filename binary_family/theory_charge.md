# Charge: general theory of the two-court renormalization curves

## Setup (self-contained)

A hidden-state world and an agent interact in discrete rounds; the public record
is an interleaved stream of action tokens and observation tokens,
h = (a1, x1, a2, x2, ...).

- World: hidden state s_t in a finite set S, controlled kernel
  T^(a,x)[s,s'] = P(x_t = x, s_{t+1} = s' | s_t = s, a_t = a).
- Agent: policy pi(a | internal state); internal state xi = (eta, z):
  eta a posterior over S (exact Bayes filtering on the realized stream),
  z a policy/identity coordinate — in the simplest case an index theta in a
  finite roster Theta, carried as a point p in the simplex Delta(Theta),
  possibly degenerate (a vertex).
- Two READERS: R+ and R- are two copies of the same exact Bayesian model (same
  kernels, same policy family), rolled forward on the SAME realized stream h',
  differing only in their state at a time t0 — R+ starts from a perturbed state
  (perturbation direction v), R- from the unperturbed one. The stream itself is
  generated by the actual system, law P*; P* may coincide with R+'s law (the
  perturbation penetrated the agent), with R-'s law (injection: emitted actions
  were forced for a finite window, the agent's state untouched), or with neither.
- The two courts: before each token, each reader emits a predictive probability
  for it. For channel c in {a, x} define
      Lambda^c_s = sum over tau <= s, y_tau in channel c, of
                   log [ R+(y_tau | h'_tau) / R-(y_tau | h'_tau) ].
  Structural fact: given the history, action-token likelihoods depend only on
  the z/identity side of a reader's state; observation-token likelihoods depend
  only on the eta/world side (up to posterior correlation between the two).

Empirically, in a fully solvable binary instance (2-state world with flip rate
lambda and consequence parameter c_w: with prob c_w the world's next state is
the agent's action; observation = state flipped w.p. q; roster of two Bernoulli
or belief-responsive policies) we observe: finite plateaus of either sign,
exactly-zero curves, linear drift to +/- infinity, and plateau totals that are
non-monotone in the channel noise q.

## Questions

1. SHAPES. Classify the possible asymptotic behaviors of Lambda^a_s and
   Lambda^x_s (almost surely, and in expectation over P*): convergence to a
   finite limit, linear drift, sublinear unbounded growth (sqrt(s)? log(s)?),
   sign changes of the running total, non-monotone expected trajectories.
   Which shapes are possible, which impossible? Proofs or explicit
   counterexample constructions.
2. LIMITS AND RATES. When a finite limit Lambda^c_inf exists: what functional
   of (perturbation, dynamics, channel) is it? What governs the approach rate
   and the fluctuation (variance) of the limit? Under linear drift, identify
   the slope.
3. CONDITIONS. Sharpest conditions you can state — on the perturbation
   (interior vs boundary of the relevant simplex), the dynamics (mixing,
   ergodicity), the channel (informativeness) — separating the regimes in (1).
4. INVARIANTS. Which statements are universal (model-free: two Bayesian
   readers, shared stream, true law P*) versus specific to families like the
   binary instance?
5. THEORY. Connect to standard machinery where it genuinely applies, citing
   the precise results used. Label every claim (i) proved, (ii) sketched,
   or (iii) conjectured.
